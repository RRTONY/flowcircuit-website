# Research Findings: Polanyi, Tacit Knowledge, and Team Dynamics

## Michael Polanyi and Tacit Knowledge
*   **Core Concept:** "We can know more than we can tell." (The Tacit Dimension, 1966).
*   **Relevance to Innovation:** Innovation often relies on *tacit knowledge*—the unwritten, intuitive know-how that resides in people's heads (e.g., a Creator's "gut feeling" about a trend, an Executor's "muscle memory" for coding).
*   **The Relay Problem:** Traditional processes (and personality tests like MBTI) focus on *explicit knowledge* (what can be documented). They fail to capture *how* tacit knowledge is transferred.
*   **The Z-Process Solution:** The Z-Process is effectively a "Tacit Knowledge Transfer Protocol." It maps the *handoff* of this intuitive knowing from one state (Abstract/Creator) to another (Concrete/Executor).

## Efficacy: Generative Roles vs. Descriptive Personality (MBTI)
*   **MBTI (Myers-Briggs):** *Descriptive*. Tells you "who you are" (e.g., INTJ). Good for self-awareness, bad for prediction. "I am an introvert" doesn't tell the team *what to do* with me in a crisis.
*   **Team Dimensions / Z-Process:** *Generative*. Tells you "what you do" (e.g., Refiner). It is *functional*. "I find flaws." This predicts *output* and *friction*.
*   **The "Why Better" Argument:**
    *   **Actionability:** MBTI is a label; Z-Process is a job description.
    *   **Flow:** MBTI maps static traits; Z-Process maps dynamic energy flow (The Relay).
    *   **Conflict Resolution:** MBTI says "we are different"; Z-Process says "we are in different parts of the cycle."
